The homologous GRAS transcription factors RAM1 and RAD1 coordinate shared and separate gene networks to regulate arbuscular mycorrhiza symbiosis

Summary

  • Arbuscular mycorrhiza (AM), a symbiosis between plants and Glomeromycotan fungi is based on the exchange of nutrients, which majorly occurs between fungal tree‐shaped arbuscules and root cortex cells. Two homologous plant GRAS transcription factors, RAM1 and RAD1, were previously reported to interact, and both were implicated in regulating arbuscule branching in Lotus japonicus . We revisited the role of L. japonicus RAM1 and RAD1 and asked whether they act together in mediating arbuscule development and gene expression, or whether they act additively.

  • We performed comprehensive genetic, phenotypic, and transcriptome analyses of two allelic Lotus ram1, rad1, and ram1 rad1 mutants; and of roots ectopically expressing RAM1 and/or RAD1.

  • We found that RAD1 is required for full root colonization in a RAM1 ‐dependent manner, but in our growth conditions, RAD1 is not required for arbuscule branching. In addition, RAM1 and RAD1 are required for activation of partially common and partially distinct sets of downstream genes.

  • Thus, in contrast to RAM1, Lotus RAD1 is not essential for arbuscule branching. Furthermore, RAM1 and RAD1 seem to act partially synergistically but seem to participate in additional alternative transcription factor complexes to regulate AM‐induced gene expression and AM development.

DNAharvester: A Nextflow Pipeline for Analysing Highly Degraded DNA from Ancient and Historical Specimens

Abstract

Motivation

Ancient DNA (aDNA) research has advanced rapidly with the development of high-throughput sequencing, enabling genome-wide analyses of large collections of prehistoric specimens. However, analysing palaeontological and archaeological material with highly degraded DNA constitutes a major bioinformatic challenge. DNA from such samples is characterized by short fragment lengths, low endogenous content, post-mortem damage, and cross-species contamination, which can increase spurious mapping and reference bias, affecting downstream population genetic inferences.

Results

We present DNAharvester, a modular and reproducible pipeline designed specifically for processing highly degraded DNA from ancient and historical specimens. DNAharvester integrates metagenomic filtering, competitive mapping, adaptive aligner selection (incorporating BWA-aln, BWA-mem, and Bowtie2), and systematic evaluation of reference bias and spurious mapping. By incorporating flexible mapping and filtering strategies, the pipeline can be adapted to varying sample preservation, focusing on maximizing authentic data recovery. DNAharvester features subworkflows for iterative assembly of mitogenomes, identification of genomic repeats and CpG sites, taxonomic classification, microbial/pathogen screening, genetic sex determination, and variant calling. To accommodate varying sequencing depths, the pipeline supports diploid variant calling, genotype likelihood estimation, and pseudo-haploid random allele calling. We validated the pipeline using simulated and empirical datasets, demonstrating its ability to systematically mitigate complex background contamination while preserving authentic genomic signals. By streamlining complex bioinformatic tasks through simple configuration files, DNAharvester establishes a standardized approach for analysing aDNA datasets and makes genomic analyses of ancient remains accessible to the broader research community.

Availability and Implementation

Implemented in Nextflow, DNAharvester provides a highly scalable, containerized framework that enhances reproducibility, portability, and robustness in aDNA analyses. DNAharvester is open source under the GNU General Public License v3.0 and freely available at https://github.com/NBISweden/DNAharvester.

Assembly-graph-assisted haplotype-resolved scaffolding of polyploid genomes

Abstract

Assembling polyploid genomes at the chromosome level remains a major challenge due to the difficulty of accurately distinguishing homologous chromosomes, leaving a vast number of polyploid species without high-quality genome sequences. Current chromatin conformation capture (3C)-based haplotype-resolved scaffolding methods often produce highly incomplete and mis-phased assemblies of complex polyploids, as short sequences lacking sufficient interaction support and collapsed regions with misleading signals are difficult to resolve. Here we present GPhase, a haplotype-resolved scaffolding algorithm that integrates 3C (for example, Hi-C, Pore-C and Omni-C) data with assembly graphs to enhance accuracy, completeness and contiguity. By leveraging graph connectivity derived from long-read overlaps, GPhase effectively overcomes key limitations of 3C. When applied to 15 complex polyploid genomes, GPhase reduced the sequence missing rate (BUSCO-based) by up to tens-fold, decreased the sequence error rate (k -mer-based) by up to an order of magnitude and improved the contiguity (N50) of gap-free sequences by several-fold compared with existing tools.

Unraveling the K+ transporter gene family in rice: evolution, subcellular localization, K+ uptake activity, and transcriptional responses to salt stress

Rice is a global staple food crop, and its yield is seriously limited by soil salinity. The high-affinity K+ transporter/K+ uptake permease/K+ transporter (HAK/KUP/KT) family of potassium transporters plays a pivotal role in K+ homeostasis and plant salt tolerance. However, their systematic characterization and functional relevance to salt tolerance in rice have not been clarified. Here, we conducted genome-wide identification and structural characterization of the HAK/KUP/KT family, assessed their expression profiles under salt stress, and examined their K+ transport function. In total, 27 OsHAK genes were identified in the rice genome, most of which exhibited salt-responsive expression. Yeast functional complementation assays confirmed that OsHAK11, OsHAK13, OsHAK23, OsHAK24, OsHAK25, and OsHAK26 can mediate K+ absorption. Among them, OsHAK11 and OsHAK25 showed relatively strong K+ uptake ability. Subcellular localization analysis revealed that OsHAK11 and OsHAK25 were located in the plasma membrane. Our research systematically analyzed the sequence characteristics of the rice HAK gene family, examined their transcriptional responses to salt stress, and further evaluated the K+ uptake capacity of HAK proteins. These results provide a foundation for further elucidating the function of OsHAKs in plant salt tolerance.

Whole-genome duplication reshapes gene–miRNA regulatory networks underlying improved salt stress tolerance in Medicago sativa

Abstract

Whole-genome duplication (WGD) is a recurrent feature of plant evolution and crop improvement, yet its direct contribution to abiotic stress tolerance remains difficult to disentangle from hybridization effects. Using full-sibling diploid (2x) and neotetraploid (4x) Medicago sativa genotypes generated through bilateral sexual polyploidization, we investigated how WGD alone reshapes physiological performance and gene–miRNA regulatory networks under salt stress. Neotetraploid plants displayed enhanced tolerance to salinity, maintaining higher chlorophyll content, stomatal conductance, and shoot integrity, alongside reduced visual damage compared with diploid siblings. Transcriptome profiling revealed that ploidy level, rather than salt treatment per se, was the dominant driver of gene expression variation. Indeed, WGD resulted in extensive and largely constitutive transcriptional reprogramming, with more than 2,000 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) distinguishing 4x from 2x plants under both control and saline conditions. By contrast, under salt stress, 705 DEGs specifically differentiated tetraploids from diploids and were enriched in receptor-like kinases, phosphorylation pathways, ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis, flavonoid biosynthesis, and WRKY- and MYB-centered regulatory modules. Co-expression network analysis identified salt-associated modules linking signaling and antioxidant pathways with stress-related physiological traits. Small RNA sequencing further showed that WGD reshapes post-transcriptional regulation, with limited salt-induced miRNA changes but the emergence of ploidy-dependent miRNA hubs, including miR2593e and a novel miRNA, targeting signaling and defense-related genes. We hypothesize that WGD establishes a stress-ready regulatory architecture in which coordinated transcriptional and post-transcriptional network rebalancing acts as a buffering system that improves alfalfa physiological performance under salinity, without strong induction of canonical salt-responsive genes.

FAMUS: A Few-Shot Learning Framework for Large-Scale Protein Annotation

Abstract

Motivation

Gene function prediction is pivotal in genomic and metagenomic data analysis. Current annotation tools typically rely on the most similar sequence from the query database and struggle to robustly set meaningful hit thresholds, causing a major challenge for reliable annotation.

Results

Here, we present a contrastive learning framework for functional annotation. FAMUS (Functional Annotation Method Using Supervised contrastive learning) compares query sequences to a full array of profile Hidden Markov Models, encoding each sequence’s complete similarity profile, rather than only its top-scoring hit, into a condensed vector space that minimizes the distance between proteins from the same family. Unannotated sequences are incorporated as negative examples during training, enabling robust detection of proteins that fall outside the reference database without requiring user-defined thresholds. FAMUS outperformed KEGG’s native KofamScan for KEGG Orthology annotation and InterPro’s InterProScan for PANTHER family annotation, especially on realistic, sparsely annotated data. We used this approach to build four protein annotation models spanning the KEGG Orthology, InterPro, OrthoDB, and EggNOG databases. FAMUS is a comprehensive, modular framework that supports both pre-defined and user-specific databases. It can be readily integrated into genomic and metagenomic analysis pipelines for accurate, large-scale functional annotation.

Availability and Implementation

All four models are available as a conda package and via our web server at https://app.famus.bursteinlab.org/, and source code is freely available on GitHub at https://github.com/burstein-lab/famus/ as well as a source code snapshot at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23132542.

Genome sequencing and de novo assembly elucidate the genetic basis of grain quality and fragrance in heirloom rice cultivar Mushk Budji

Background

Mushk Budji is a premium, GI-tagged, heritage rice landrace of Kashmir valley, J&amp;K, India, distinguished by its short-bold grains, strong aroma, and soft texture. While its morphological and biochemical traits have been documented, no dedicated genomic resource has previously been available. Here we report a de novo genome assembly of Mushk Budji with adequate depth.

Methods

We first-time performed reference guided whole-genome sequencing of Mushk Budji using the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform, generating 64.97 Gb of raw data. The genome assembly was achieved using SPAdes and scaffolded using RagTag against the Nipponbare reference. Structural and functional annotations were conducted with AUGUSTUS and curated across NCBI, UniProt, and KEGG databases. The haplotype analysis was performed for loci related to quality using a set of contrasting genotypes.

Results and discussion

The final assembly comprised 378.29 Mb with an N50 of 29.24 Mb and 97.0% BUSCO completeness. A total of 43,434 non-redundant protein-coding genes were identified, with significant enrichment in pathways related to aroma (2-acetyl-1-pyrroline) and starch biosynthesis. Allelic profiling confirmed the characteristic 8-bp deletion in Badh2, conferring fragrance, alongside additional genes shaping quality and adaptability. Unique haplotypes at SSI and RAmy1A, resembling those of temperate japonica variety Koshihikari, were associated with distinct soft and cohesive cooked texture of Mushk Budji . Comparative analyses revealed evolutionary divergence from canonical japonica references at specific loci. Selective signature analyses revealed contrasting evolutionary patterns among major quality genes. SSI showed evidence of balancing selection, whereas GS3 and Badh2 exhibited signatures of strong human-mediated selection, reflecting the historical preference for desirable grain quality and fragrance traits. Genes controlling amylose content and cooking quality (Wx and Alk) were under moderate purifying selection, indicating functional optimization.

Conclusion

The genomics resource generated in the present study establishes Mushk Budji as a distinct temperate japonica landrace with unique allelic combinations underpinning its aroma, texture and adaptability. The dataset provides a foundation for conservation, breeding and functional genomics, ensuring the sustainable utilization of this heritage rice.

Chloroplast Genome Evolution, Heteroplasmy, and Inverted Repeat Dynamics in the Elymus Complex (Triticeae, Poaceae): Insights from Single-Molecule Sequencing of Elymus ciliaris and Comparative Analysis of St-Genome Lineages

ABSTRACT

Background/Objectives

Elymus sensu lato (Poaceae) is arguably the largest and most complex genus in the tribe Triticeae. It includes hybrids and polyploids based on x=7 chromosomes, all including the St genome, forming a valuable genepool for forage grass and cereal breeding. Analysis of chloroplast genome diversity and structural dynamics is critical for resolving maternal lineages, reticulate evolution and biodiversity across this agronomically important complex, refining their taxonomy, conservation and exploitation.

Methods

We sequenced the complete chloroplast genome (plastome) of Elymus ciliaris (4x=2n=28; StStYY) using ultra-long Oxford Nanopore single-molecule reads and compared it to 77 additional chloroplast genomes representing major St-genome lineages in Elymus s.l. (Pseudoroegneria St; Elymus s.s. StH, StY; Thinopyrum StJ/E; Campeiostachys StYH; Kengyilia StYP). We analyzed structure, nucleotide diversity, inverted repeat (IR) dynamics, and phylogenetic signal.

Results

The E. ciliaris chloroplast genome was 135,008 bp long (38.3% GC) with a canonical quadripartite structure. Single-molecule reads (n=81) revealed heteroplasmy: two Small-Single-Copy (SSC) orientations at 30%:70% frequency, indicating an inversion polymorphism. Across the Elymus group, comparative analysis of chloroplast assemblies showed high structural conservation but lineage-specific IR-boundary shifts. Kengyilia exhibits exceptional IR expansion. Nucleotide diversity hotspots localize to the large single-copy region, especially in StY lineages. Phylogenies recover a monophyletic St-containing clade but do not delineate genera, reflecting reticulate evolution.

Conclusions

Single-molecule sequencing uncovered heteroplasmy with an inversion polymorphism in a single plant of Elymus ciliaris, hidden in short read assemblies. There were no other polymorphisms, as expected for chloroplast sequences. Our analyses showed that a Pseudoroegneria-like St chloroplast genome predominates as the maternal donor across Elymus polyploids. Variable regions and IR dynamics offer strong models for chloroplast genome evolution in reticulate lineages and suggest exploiting plastome variation to complement nuclear biodiversity studies.

Diffusible signals from Azospirillum brasilense promote rice growth and induce distinct gene expression changes in plant roots

Plants form associations with diverse beneficial microbes, including arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi, rhizobia, and plant growth-promoting bacteria (PGPB). Research on legume-rhizobia symbiosis (LRS) and AM symbiosis has demonstrated that molecular communication between symbiotic partners is essential for initiating these interactions. Genetic and biochemical studies have further identified both plant and microbial signals, as well as host genetic pathways, that regulate these interactions. However, most studies on plant-PGPB interactions have focused on direct application of PGPB to plant roots, leaving the early signaling mechanisms underlying these interactions poorly understood. In this study, we report that diffusible signals from A. brasilense can promote rice growth without direct contact between bacteria and plant roots, 7 days post-treatment (dpt). Using RNA sequencing, we identified 2,516 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in rice roots in response to diffusible signals from A. brasilense at 1 dpt. We identified differential expression of genes involved in flavonoid synthesis, defense responses, receptor activity, transcription factors, and hormone pathways. Comparative transcriptomic analysis of rice roots with or without contact with A. brasilense revealed both shared and unique gene expression patterns. We observed upregulation of certain defense-response genes (PAL, chitinase, thionin), transcription factors (AP2/ERFs, MYBs, WRKYs, and GRAS), and hormone-related (auxin and ethylene) genes without bacterial contact, but downregulation or no differential expression of these genes upon bacterial contact. This pattern suggests that initial induction followed by subsequent suppression of these genes may be crucial for establishing the interaction with A. brasilense. Our transcriptomic analysis also suggests that flavonoids may be required throughout the interaction, whereas nitrate transporters appear to be involved at later stages and likely require contact between plant roots and bacteria. Collectively, our findings reveal that diffusible signals from A. brasilense can promote rice growth and induce distinct gene expression changes in plant roots. These results provide novel insights into the early phase of rice- A. brasilense interaction and highlight the potential for leveraging plant-PGPB signaling mechanisms to enhance crop growth under adverse environmental conditions.

Genome-wide association mapping reveals stable genomic regions associated with fruit quality traits in tomato (Solanum lycopersicum L.)

Fruit quality has become a primary breeding objective in tomato because it determines consumer acceptance, processing efficiency, and market value. However, quality-related traits are quantitatively inherited and strongly influenced by environmental conditions, making genetic improvement challenging. In this study, genome-wide association studies (GWAS) was conducted to elucidate the genetic architecture of six economically important fruit quality traits, including luminosity, hue, chroma, soluble solids content (°Brix), titratable acidity, and fruit fresh weight (FW), using a diverse panel of 305 tomato accessions evaluated over two growing seasons. Fruit weight, luminosity, hue, chroma, and °Brix exhibited high broad-sense heritability, whereas titratable acidity showed moderate heritability. Correlation analysis revealed strong positive relationships among color-related traits, while °Brix was positively associated with acidity but negatively correlated with fruit color attributes and FW. Skim sequencing generated 4,618 high-quality single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), and GWAS was performed using multiple models. GWAS identified 15 non-redundant marker-trait associations (MTAs) across chromosomes, with several loci consistently detected across statistical models.
